How Aarogya-Vāyu works

Build with AI: Code for Communities • Track 3: Smart Health & Supply Chain Resilience

When smog or a heatwave hits, breathing and dehydration cases rise within days. Some rural clinics run out of medicine while a clinic nearby has spare stock that may expire. Aarogya-Vāyu finds those clinics and proposes a transfer; the district's Chief Medical Officer approves it.

  1. 1. Read the weather. Live air quality, heat, rainfall and minimum temperature (Open-Meteo, Copernicus CAMS) raise the expected demand for each medicine: smog for breathing medicines, heat and floods for ORS and fever medicine, cold for antibiotics. The Conditions buttons switch to a smog, heatwave, flood, cold-wave or normal demo, for your browser only.
  2. 2. Clinics report stock. In Report stock by voice, try a sample or press the mic. Gemini picks out the clinic, medicine and count.
  3. 3. Find a transfer. Press Find stock next to a clinic, or Run Agent Pipeline. Gemini calls the transfer solver; the solver picks the donor and quantity (within 35 km, keeping 14 days at the donor, oldest batch first).
  4. 4. Doctor approves. Under Waiting for your approval, press Approve & Reserve Stock. A dispatch note is issued and the step is written to the audit log.
Also on the left menu
  • Clinic: one clinic's own screen: stock on the shelf, deliveries coming in and going out.
  • Ask: questions in plain language, such as "Which clinics will run out first?"
  • Photo: Gemini counts boxes in a shelf photo and compares the count with the register.
  • Audit: every action is chained with SHA-256, so editing any past entry makes verification fail.

Clinic inventories are sample data; air quality and weather are live.
